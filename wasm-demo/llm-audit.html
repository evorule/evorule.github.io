What would an attacker actually have to do? (the honest cost breakdown)

Suppose someone wants to rewrite the model's reply you just recorded. Step by step:

  1. Edit the fact's text — trivial. This is the button above. Detected immediately: the recorded content_hash no longer matches BLAKE3 of the edited text.
  2. Re-chain the entire tail — mechanically easy, since BLAKE3 is public: recompute every later entry's link off the new tip. After this step the chain is internally consistent again — and in this demo, undistinguishable. That is exactly why the demo is honest about being a mechanism demo: an in-browser chain with no external anchor cannot, by itself, prove history.
  3. Defeat every anchor outside the system — the step that makes tampering expensive in production. The real engine seals each fact into an append-only WAL whose hash chain is re-verified on load, exposes /audit/export (chain metadata) and /audit/verify, and a re-chain changes the chain tip — so any independently kept tip (a monitor, an export archive, another machine's copy) exposes the rewrite. Anchors must all be rewritten in the same window, without any verifier noticing.
  4. Explain the rewritten history — the step that cannot be hashed away. Audits are about accountability: a chain whose tip moved is itself evidence that something was re-chained, and the production system's verification reports make the movement visible.

So the honest claim is: tampering is detectable, cheaply and immediately, at the first edited byte; making a tamper stick requires re-chaining the tail and silently defeating every out-of-system anchor — a conspiracy, not an edit.

What this demo shows — and what it does not claim. This page demonstrates tamper-evident record-keeping: any edit to recorded content is detectable, and the demo lets you commit that edit yourself. It does not claim "tamper-proof": integrity in production additionally depends on deployment discipline (append-only WAL, verifier uptime, anchoring of the chain tip outside the system). The conversation above is scripted so the demo is byte-for-byte reproducible — the hash chain, causal links, transcript facts and offline verifier are the real mechanism, compiled from the AGPL source linked below. An attacker who edits one fact must re-chain the entire tail (and, in production, defeat out-of-system anchoring) to restore consistency.
